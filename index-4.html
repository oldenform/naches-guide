The people behind the valley

Stories worth remembering

Naches is more than a stop along the highway. Its character comes from generations of people who served, worked, farmed, volunteered and helped shape the community.

Early Naches history

George Grahlfs

Early Naches name • World War I serviceman

George Grahlfs is part of the early history of Naches and is remembered in the community's historical record. He was among the Naches-area men who served in the U.S. Army during World War I.

Naches history
Community service

Ted Cowan

1928–2026 • Volunteer • Fire chief • Community builder

Ted Cowan devoted decades to Naches. His community work included the Lions Club, Sportsman's Days, the community swimming pool, Applewood Park and the Naches Fire Department, where he eventually served as chief.

Get outside

Top-Rated Hiking Trails

Naches Peak Loop

★ 4.8 (653) · 3.5–4.5 mi loop · Easy–moderate

Starts near Tipsoo Lake. Wildflowers late July–early August, sweeping Mt. Rainier views.

Sheep Lake & Sourdough Gap

★ 4.7 (110) · Moderate, steeper near the gap

Follows the PCT off SR-410. Alpine lake, swimming, big views from the gap.

Boulder Cave Trail

★ 4.5 (276) · ~1.5–2 mi · Reservation required

Walk-through lava-tube cave; bring a light layer and flashlight.

Snow Mountain Ranch

★ 4.7 (122) · Multiple loops, easy–difficult

Choose-your-own-hike network of trails just outside Yakima; watch for rattlesnakes in summer.

Tieton Nature Trail Suspension Bridge

★ 4.7 (33) · ~0.5 mi round trip · Easy

Short walk near Goose Prairie to a suspension bridge over the Tieton River and basalt columns.

License Plate Reader Information

A professional, neutral reference for visitors and residents interested in automated license plate reader (ALPR) systems in the Naches area.

Public information

The available local reporting indicates that an ALPR camera has been reported in the Naches area. Yakima County and Washington State Patrol do not publish an official public site-by-site camera map comparable to some municipal camera programs.

The DeFlock project provides a community-reported national map that can be used as a supplemental reference for reported Flock Safety cameras in Washington.

Washington framework

Washington's 2026 ALPR law establishes requirements for agency use and registration of ALPR systems. For current legal or agency information, consult the relevant official government source.
